RoboRacer Core Kit

The RoboRacer Core Kit eliminates complexity by delivering a fully assembled, pre-tested vehicle for 1:10-scale autonomous racing. Description

Building an autonomous racecar shouldn’t start with weeks of sourcing parts, debugging hardware, or resolving integration issues. The RoboRacer Core Kit exists to remove that friction. We handle component selection, procurement, assembly, wiring, and validation of the entire autonomy stack—so teams can focus on algorithms, performance, and innovation. By delivering a fully assembled, pre-tested system that mounts directly onto your chosen chassis, RoboRacer enables faster onboarding, predictable performance, and a reliable foundation for education, research, and competitive autonomous racing.

Academic programs and research labs often lose valuable time and resources assembling and troubleshooting autonomous vehicle hardware instead of advancing coursework or research outcomes. The RoboRacer Core Kit addresses this by delivering a fully assembled, pre-tested vehicle, with all sensors and compute integrated and validated. Because every component is a standard, openly documented part—a Traxxas chassis, a VESC controller, a Hokuyo scanner, an NVIDIA Jetson module—the platform stays flexible while significantly reducing procurement complexity, setup time, and technical risk. Faculty and procurement teams benefit from predictable performance, faster deployment into labs or classrooms, standardized platforms across cohorts, and reduced dependency on specialized hardware integration expertise.

ROBORACER Program

What is the RoboRacer Program?

RoboRacer is an international community of researchers, engineers, and autonomous systems enthusiasts. It aims to foster interest, excitement, and critical thinking about the rapidly growing field of autonomous systems and robotics. Through collaboration between academic institutions, engineers, and innovators, RoboRacer enables teams to develop, test, and improve autonomous racing technologies on standardized platforms.

The programme is widely known by its earlier name, F1TENTH — its open-source ROS 2 packages and the f1tenth_gym simulator are still published under the f1tenth GitHub organisation, and its head-to-head Autonomous Grand Prix events run alongside robotics conferences such as IEEE ICRA and IROS. Building to that shared 1:10-scale specification is what lets code, maps and published results move between institutions.

Ambimat Electronics in collaboration with global research partners has designed the Power Supply board for autonomous racing systems.

What’s in the box

Major Parts Included

Every item arrives mounted, wired and tested together as one vehicle — not as a box of components to integrate.

  • Jetson Orin Super Nano Onboard compute — Ampere GPU, CUDA and TensorRT for perception and control
  • MK IV VESC Field-oriented motor control, steering, wheel odometry and onboard IMU
  • MDF Base Plate Laser-cut equipment deck carrying the compute, LiDAR and power hardware
  • Hokuyo LiDAR 270° planar laser scan over Ethernet — the input to SLAM and obstacle avoidance
  • 3S Battery (for India only) 11.1 V lithium-polymer pack powering both traction and electronics
  • Battery Charger (for India only) Balance charger for the 3S pack
  • Traxxas Slash VXL 4x4 Chassis 1:10-scale four-wheel-drive platform with Velineon brushless drive
  • Power Distribution Unit Ambimat AE170 board — regulated 19 V, 12 V and 5 V rails from one input

Frequently asked questions

What is RoboRacer?

RoboRacer is an international community of researchers, engineers and autonomous systems enthusiasts built around 1:10-scale autonomous racing — the programme widely known by its earlier name, F1TENTH. RoboRacer is also the name of the autonomous racing platform Ambimat Electronics builds for that specification, sold as the RoboRacer Core Kit.

What is included in the RoboRacer Core Kit?

An NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano Super compute module, a VESC 6 MkIV motor controller, a Hokuyo 2D LiDAR, the Ambimat AE170 power distribution unit, a Traxxas Slash VXL 4x4 chassis, an MDF base plate, a 3S lithium-polymer battery and a charger — all assembled, wired and tested together. Loading of the Jetson operating system, complete ROS stack configuration and 60 days of remote technical support make up Software Setup / Technical Support, which is included in the INR 628,000 + GST option.

Is RoboRacer the same as F1TENTH?

They describe the same platform lineage. F1TENTH is the 1:10-scale autonomous racing project the community grew from, and its open-source code still lives under the f1tenth GitHub organisation; RoboRacer is the name the community and its Autonomous Grand Prix events now use. The RoboRacer Core Kit is built to that shared vehicle specification, so community code runs on it without a hardware redesign.

What software does the RoboRacer Core Kit run?

Ubuntu 22.04 LTS with NVIDIA JetPack, which brings CUDA, cuDNN and TensorRT, and ROS 2 Humble Hawksbill. Where Software Setup / Technical Support is included, the LiDAR and VESC drivers are already configured on delivery. Teams build with colcon, write nodes in rclpy or rclcpp, visualise in RViz2 and record runs with ros2 bag.

Who uses the RoboRacer Core Kit?

Universities teaching autonomy and control courses at cohort scale, RoboRacer and F1TENTH competition teams preparing for Autonomous Grand Prix events, and robotics research laboratories that need a reproducible hardware baseline for SLAM, model-predictive control, reinforcement learning and multi-agent experiments. The use-cases page covers each in detail.

Both routes are on the contact and order page. In India the Core Kit is INR 580,000 + GST, or INR 628,000 + GST with Software Setup / Technical Support, with flat-rate shipping to any destination within India at INR 4,500 + GST. Internationally the complete kit is US$5,639 with flat shipping at US$500. Full detail is in the pricing section.
